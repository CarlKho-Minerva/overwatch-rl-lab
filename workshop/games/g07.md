# Game 7: Counter-Pick
**Lecture 7:** Policy Gradient. **You play:** the opponent of a learning bot, picking a hero each round (as Soldier: 76, standing still). **Time:** about 5 minutes.

## The idea
A bot learns odds for its moves directly, without scoring them: three preferences become three odds (a softmax), it draws its pick, then makes what worked more likely.

## The Overwatch metaphor
Counter-picking. Here Tracer beats Widowmaker, Widowmaker beats Pharah, Pharah beats Tracer; the screen says this is the game's rule, not a claim about real Overwatch.

## How to play
Fifteen rounds. The bot locks in hidden, then you pick: left click Tracer, right click Widowmaker, melee Pharah. Bars on the right show its odds. Phase A: one hero of your choice, six rounds. Phase B: a fresh bot, free play, five rounds at nudge size 0.6, four at 2.5, showing how much a perfect counter-picker would win against your mix. Five predictions come between the phases.

## What to notice
Against one hero the counter's bar climbs. A third each leaves nothing to learn. At nudge 2.5 one lucky round sends a bar near 100%, where the surprise term is tiny, so it sticks.

## The same thing in reinforcement-learning words
A softmax policy trained by REINFORCE with a baseline: preference j changes by step size x (reward minus baseline) x (1 if j was picked, else 0, minus its probability). The baseline is a running average. Rock-paper-scissors is the lecture's case for a random-on-purpose optimal policy.

## What it leaves out
One situation and one-round episodes, so the return is one reward: a bandit, not the island's walk. No critic, no traces. The opponent is a person who adapts. The aliased gridworld is told in one coach line, not played. Seed change: Phase A holds you to the hero you pick first instead of naming one.

## Log lines
- `arena`: dummies made, learner on team 1
- `pick`: round, phase (0 A, 1 B, 2 B big step), your pick, draw k (u = (k + 0.5) / 1000), bot pick, bot reward
- `odds`: round, three odds before, step size, baseline before
- `pref`: round, three preferences after, baseline after
- `mix`: round, your phase B frequencies, exploitability of your mix, of the bot's odds
- `reset`: rounds so far, step size
- `score`: your wins, bot wins, draws, timed-out picks
