# Game 5: Slippery Boots
**Lecture 5:** Model-Free Control. **You play:** Tracer, crossing high ground next to a drop. **Time:** about 5 minutes.

## The idea
On a calm day the fastest route hugs the edge. Then a Lucio shows up.

Judge the edge two ways: by how you move, boops and all, or by your best possible walk. Next to a drop, the two disagree.

## The Overwatch metaphor
Holding a ledge with an enemy Lucio around: do you plan for the boops you will take, or for the player you wish you were?

## How to play
Beside the lecture's page: read a stop, Alt+Tab in, and the game waits there (STOP k OF 6 beside the title). Nothing times out. WASD walks; left click, right click or melee answer. Before each crossing, left click picks the EDGE lane (11 steps beside the drop), right click the INLAND lane (13 steps, one pad in). Each pad costs 1, the goal 50, a fall 100. From stop 2, on one step in ten Lucio boops you one pad in a random direction instead.

1. A calm crossing: which lane pays more?
2. Lucio arrives: the chance one edge step ends over the drop.
3. How often the edge lane ends in a fall.
4. The long run: which lane earns more with boops.
5. Which learner prefers the edge (back to lecture 4's TD).
6. Debrief.

## What to notice
Without boops the edge pays 39, inland 37. With them the edge averages 0.41 (a fall 26 times in 100), inland 30.31.

One boop in ten, and the best lane is nearly worthless.

## The same thing in reinforcement-learning words
The boop is epsilon-greedy, epsilon 0.1, four moves: 0.025 per step toward the drop.

SARSA learns about the walker you are (on-policy): its target uses the move you really make next, so it picks inland. Q-learning learns about the walker you wish you were (off-policy): its target uses the best next move, never a boop, so it values the edge at 39.

## What it leaves out
In the lecture the random move is the learner's own exploring; here Lucio makes it. (A boop belonging to the world would be learned by Q-learning too.) No learner runs: the values are policy evaluation of two fixed lane walkers on a 4 by 12 grid. Silver shows the cliff walk only on a slide.
