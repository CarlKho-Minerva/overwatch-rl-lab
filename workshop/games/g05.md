# Game 5: Slippery Boots
**Lecture 5:** Model-Free Control. **You play:** Tracer, crossing high ground next to a drop. **Time:** about 5 minutes.

## The idea
Learn about how you actually act, slips included (on-policy, SARSA), or about the best way while acting another way (off-policy, Q-learning). Next to a drop they disagree.

## The Overwatch metaphor
Holding a ledge while an enemy Lucio is around. Do you plan for the boops you will take, or for the player you wish you were?

## How to play
WASD walks; clicks or melee answer. Four crossings, white ring to green ring. Before each, left click picks the EDGE lane (11 steps, all from pads next to the drop), right click the INLAND lane (13 steps, one pad in). Each pad stepped onto costs 1, the goal pays 50, a fall costs 100. From crossing 2, on one step in ten Lucio boops you: the step becomes one of the four moves at random, from the pad you left. The right side lists each return.

## What to notice
Without boops the edge pays 39, inland 37. With them the edge averages 0.41 (a fall 26 times in 100), inland 30.31. Five predictions; question 5 reaches back to lecture 4's TD target.

## The same thing in reinforcement-learning words
The boop is epsilon-greedy, epsilon 0.1, four moves: 0.025 per step toward the drop, as for the night run's bots. SARSA's target uses the move you really make next, so it values the booped walker and picks inland. Q-learning's uses the best next move, never a boop, so it values the edge at 39.

## What it leaves out
In the lecture the random move is the learner's own exploring; here Lucio makes it. A boop belonging to the world would be learned by Q-learning too. No learner runs: the values are policy evaluation of two fixed lane walkers on a 4 by 12 grid. Silver shows the cliff walk only on a slide.
