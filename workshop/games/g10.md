# Game 10: The Peek
**Lecture 10:** Classic Games. **You play:** Tracer, peeking a Widowmaker bot. **Time:** about 6 minutes.

## The idea
Against a fixed habit the best answer is one pure move. Against a learner, any pattern is punished; the safe plan is a mix that makes reading you worthless.

## The Overwatch metaphor
Peeking a Widowmaker: the fast left flank or the safe right corner, while she scopes one. What you win, she loses.

## How to play
Played beside the lecture 10 page: read a stop there, Alt+Tab in, and the game waits at that stop. LEFT CLICK peeks left, RIGHT CLICK right; she picks at once. Left: +3 clean, -2 caught. Right: +1 or -1. A red beam means caught. Nothing times out.
1. Peek a Widowmaker who never moves (question, 4 peeks).
2. Peek a Widowmaker who learns you (question, 5 peeks).
3. Find the peek she cannot read (2 questions).
4. Peek by the die (question, 5 peeks).
5. Start two learners (question; F starts 300 bot peeks).
6. Debrief (F).

## What to notice
The left habit that beat the fixed Widowmaker is what the learner punishes first. The safe mix peeks the better corner 2 times in 7 and earns 1/7 a peek whatever she does. In self-play choices flip 12 times; averages end near 29% and 57%.

## The same thing in reinforcement-learning words
Best response: the best plan against a fixed opponent. Minimax: the mix that guarantees the most against the worst opponent; 1/7 is the value. Nash equilibrium: policies where neither side gains by changing alone. The learner answers your average (fictitious play) half the time, else repeats her own, as in Smooth UCT. Self-play: two learners moving each other's target.

## What it leaves out
One 2x2 matrix game, replayed: no search tree, no positions, no TD. Her learning is counting.
