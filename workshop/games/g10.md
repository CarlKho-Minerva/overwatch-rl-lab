# Game 10: The Peek
**Lecture 10:** Classic Games. **You play:** Tracer, peeking a Widowmaker bot. **Time:** about 5 minutes.

## The idea
Against a fixed habit the best answer is one pure move. Against someone who learns your habits, any pattern is punished, and the safe plan is a mix that makes reading you worthless.

## The Overwatch metaphor
Peeking a Widowmaker: you swing the fast left flank or the safe right corner, she holds her scope on one. What you win, she loses.

## How to play
LEFT CLICK peeks left, RIGHT CLICK right; she picks at the same moment. Left: +3 if she is not there, -2 if she is. Right: +1 or -1. You are moved (not walked) to your corner, she turns to hers, a red beam means caught. Fourteen peeks: 4 against a Widowmaker who always holds right, 5 against one who learns, 5 with a die saying LEFT 2 times in 7. Then two learning bots play 300 peeks on the bars. Six predictions.

## What to notice
The always-left habit that beat the fixed Widowmaker is what the learning one punishes first. The safe mix peeks the better corner 2 times in 7 and earns 1/7 a peek whatever she does. In self-play the choices flip 12 times, yet the averages end near 29% and 57%.

## The same thing in reinforcement-learning words
Best response: the best policy against a fixed opponent. Minimax: the mix that guarantees the most against the worst opponent; 1/7 is the value. Nash equilibrium: policies where neither side gains by changing alone. The learning Widowmaker answers your average (fictitious play) half the time and repeats her own average otherwise, as in Smooth UCT. Self-play: two learners moving each other's target.

## What it leaves out
One 2x2 matrix game, replayed: no search tree, no turns, no positions, no TD. Her learning is counting. Five die peeks are too few to see 1/7.
